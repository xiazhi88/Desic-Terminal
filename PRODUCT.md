# Product

## Product Name

Desic Terminal

## Register

product

## Users

Active crypto derivatives traders and analysts who use a desktop terminal for continuous market monitoring, chart analysis, order management, and AI-assisted research or execution.

## Product Purpose

Provide a fast, dependable OKX perpetual trading workspace where realtime market data, durable news and smart-money intelligence, local historical data, charting, account operations, and an AI trading assistant work as one coherent desktop tool.

Market Intelligence news is stored locally as durable article and event evidence. The news workspace supports local-day navigation, bounded pagination, and per-device read cursors; background refreshes count only articles first seen after the cursor, so repeated synchronization does not create false unread alerts.

The Current Orders and Historical Orders workspaces group orders by exchange semantics rather than by a generic ordinary/strategy split: limit/market, advanced limit, take-profit/stop-loss, trailing protection, and trigger orders each have a dedicated tab. Iceberg, TWAP, or unknown algo types remain visible in an additional tab when present. Current categories retain their native amend, cancel, status, and price-field behavior; historical categories combine durable ordinary-order history with the separately synchronized algo-order history without inventing missing price or PnL fields. Opening orders accept the size either in contracts or as cost in USDT, where cost is margin plus fee over the notional at the selected leverage; the ticket back-solves the contract quantity, steps it down to the contract lot, and shows the fee-inclusive cost estimate.

The main chart offers an Order Flow mode built only from data the desktop actually holds. A volume profile with point of control and 70% value area is aggregated in SQLite from local confirmed 1-minute candles across the visible range, with coverage shown. A sub-pane plots per-bucket taker buy-minus-sell delta and its cumulative CVD from the Market Intelligence taker-volume series (5-minute buckets for charts up to 15 minutes, hourly otherwise), and marks price/CVD divergences only between confirmed swing pivots. Liquidation prints recorded by the intelligence collector appear as circles sized by quantity. Current large resting walls are detected from the live 400-level book of the selected instrument and drawn from the moment they were first seen; they are a present-state reading, not a history, and the view never labels support or resistance. In this mode the order-book column shows a mirrored depth profile and ±0.3% resting imbalance.

AI position management treats stop-loss protection and take-profit exit as separate decisions. Existing-position take-profit uses an explicit close opportunity with `exitKind=take_profit` and a reduce-only limit or market exit; stop-loss uses `exitKind=stop_loss` with a trigger or market exit. A run may leave the target empty when no evidence-based target exists, but it must state that decision and the next reevaluation condition rather than silently treating a stop as a complete exit plan. Before creating a new exit, AI reconciles exact order identities and semantic roles; changing an existing target or stop uses an amend opportunity on that order, while multiple exits are reserved for explicitly documented, non-overlapping staged quantities.

## Current Market Radar Boundary

Market Radar is the independent cross-market discovery and ranking workspace for live OKX USDT perpetuals. It does not depend on watchlist membership: the product directory comes from OKX instruments data, while a bounded all-tickers snapshot supplies current price, 24-hour change, turnover, spread, and lightweight activity evidence. Stock-category instruments are enriched with searchable security names and listing exchanges from the official NASDAQ Trader Symbol Directory. Chinese interfaces additionally resolve Simplified or Traditional Chinese entity labels from Wikidata by exact ticker and listing exchange; localized matches are cached independently, refreshed as new instruments appear, and fall back through the official English name to the OKX contract code. The desktop refreshes the market snapshot adaptively rather than subscribing every contract to high-frequency ticker, trade, book, or candle streams. Opening a chart and adding a contract to the watchlist remain separate actions.

Market Radar prepares a layered local research baseline without extending watchlist-grade 1-minute storage to the full market. Live USDT perpetuals receive up to 400 days of UTC-boundary `1Dutc` history; the top 100 contracts by current USDT turnover additionally receive 90 days of `1H` history. Stock perpetual return research uses a separately identified forward-adjusted daily series. Built-in ranking combines explainable daily relative strength, realized volatility, turnover change, and trend stability with a smaller current-snapshot component. Data coverage, freshness, and fallback to snapshot-only scoring remain visible. Rankings prioritize research; they are not profit forecasts, trading advice, strategy signals, or order commands. The advanced factor expression workbench is an expert research layer and is not a primary Strategy Research tab.

Market Radar also offers a Constellation view of the same ranking: every live USDT perpetual is placed by the in-frame percentiles of two selectable factors (composite, strength, low volatility, activity, trend stability), colored by 24-hour change, sized by turnover, and highlighted only when its composite rank moved materially within six hours. Hovering shows rank and 1h/1d/7d rank changes, clicking selects the market in the existing detail panel, and a Shift-drag lasso adds up to four markets to the existing comparison. A replay timeline reads persisted hourly snapshots over 7, 30, or 90 days at 1-, 4-, or 12-hour steps; hours without a snapshot are drawn as gaps and are never interpolated. Hourly snapshots are recorded at application level while the desktop app runs, reusing its existing all-market ticker refresh, so they no longer depend on the Radar workspace being open.

AI Research keeps the center-panel research and market-intelligence shortcuts in a small vertical rail on the panel's right edge; selecting either shortcut opens the right inspector even when it is closed. The desktop retains one replaceable research snapshot per UTC hour for 90 days. These snapshots provide 1-hour, 1-day, and 7-day rank changes, weighted score-change attribution, deterministic saved filters, transition-based research alerts, and a shared point-in-time record for comparison and validation. Alerts are deduplicated, cooled down, and capped; they only publish research notifications and cannot place orders. Historical validation evaluates saved point-in-time universes against later confirmed daily candles at 1, 5, and 20 days, with Rank IC, top/bottom quantile spreads, a saved-spread cost proxy, turnover, chronological training/validation segments, and BTC-defined market regimes. It never reconstructs prior universes from today's surviving instruments. Natural-language filtering is compiled into visible deterministic clauses; unsupported text remains visible instead of being used as an opaque market decision.

AI can inspect this persisted research state through the built-in `market-radar-research` Skill and read-only `radar.*` tools. Broad current-market analysis or market-overview requests without one named instrument route first through persisted breadth and default composite ranking; real-time claims add live evidence and remain timestamp-distinct from the hourly Radar snapshot. The tool boundary exposes ranking, one-instrument evidence, 2-4 market comparison, category breadth, rank history, point-in-time validation, and saved deterministic filter definitions. Results always retain snapshot time, model version, universe coverage, ranking basis, global rank versus scoped rank, and limitations; they read SQLite directly and never infer state from the current UI tab. Radar AI tools cannot write snapshots, save filters, change alerts, create strategies or opportunities, submit orders, or modify account state.

## Current Systematic Research Boundary

Systematic Research is a local, single-contract strategy research workspace for eligible USDT perpetuals. Its scheduler invokes Python `on_bar` only after a confirmed 1-minute K-line, while strategy code can read current-time-bounded `1m`, `3m`, `5m`, `15m`, `30m`, `1H`, `2H`, `4H`, `6H`, `12H`, and `1D` series. A higher-timeframe final bar may be explicitly marked incomplete and contains only information available at that minute. New strategy creation requires an explicit template choice: a minimal no-trade blank entrypoint or a bundled research starter; the create action no longer silently inserts trading logic. Users can version Python packages with immutable saved `ctx.params`, current virtual-account state, open orders, fills, closed trades, and funding records. A save creates a new immutable version only when the name, description, source, parameters, or desktop-owned tuning ranges changed. The version history supports paged inspection and comparison, can load a historical snapshot only into the current unsaved draft, and can start a new backtest or Profile from an exact historical version. A package returns one high-level market or limit opening/closing action, an order cancellation, a no-action, or a position-protection action; it cannot access credentials, query arbitrary data, or submit an exchange order. Desktop-owned numeric parameter ranges are editable only in the desktop tuning workbench, are fixed when a run starts, and cannot be declared by source code.

### Parameter tuning workbench

Parameter tuning uses a dedicated workbench instead of exposing the optimization action beside raw JSON. The workbench recommends signal parameters, keeps protection and sizing parameters opt-in, validates the current value against each range, and offers fixed Fast/Standard/Deep budgets of 30, 100, or 300 candidate groups. Starting a run saves the current draft as an immutable research version, pins that version to the run, uses the fixed 70/30 train-validation split, and samples oversized grids deterministically so the same version and ranges reproduce the same candidate set. The optimizer reuses loaded K-line and contract data, keeps one Python process per CPU-aware worker lane, and reports progress, elapsed/estimated remaining time, and cancellation state. Completed results include the baseline validation Calmar, sampling mode, candidate budget, and the saved strategy version; adopting a result immediately creates the next strategy version and opens its backtest settings. Results open as a parameter terrain: any two tuned parameters form the axes, measured candidates are drawn as points over a visually labeled interpolation of validation Calmar (hidden parameters are collapsed by best or median, as stated on the chart), an overfit layer marks candidates whose training result is far stronger than validation, and a selected candidate shows its measured-neighbor robustness, train-versus-validation metrics, and per-parameter sensitivity, so a narrow validation spike can be told apart from a stable plateau. Candidates move from queued to running to a terminal state, and running candidates are highlighted live.

Backtests pin the Python strategy version, local K-line snapshot, virtual initial equity, preloaded confirmed history before the formal evaluation start, leverage (1x-50x), safety-margin multiplier, conservative fees/slippage, and end-of-run policy. Formal evaluation defaults to the latest 30 days ending at least one hour before the current time, and supports a maximum 366-day one-minute range; preload remains visible to the first decision but never enters equity, replay, PnL, drawdown, or statistics. Market actions fill at the next minute open. Limit actions enter a pending-order state at that open and then use only later 1-minute OHLCV traversal plus a volume-participation cap, so their results are explicitly a conservative K-line estimate rather than an order-book-queue simulation. Pending limits can partially fill, remain open, be cancelled, or expire. A full simulated close clears protection only after it fully fills; a partial close retains it. When remaining virtual margin or the configured entry budget cannot reach the contract minimum, the backtest records an opening `no_action` with the sizing reason and continues instead of failing the whole run. The virtual-margin exhaustion rule is deliberately conservative and is not an exchange liquidation-price estimate. Results preserve a complete local ledger and candle-by-candle replay with fill labels, point-in-time virtual balance and margin, current/historical positions, reproducible statistics, total elapsed time, and persisted phase diagnostics. Long replays initially load a bounded recent candle page; moving the replay timeline loads the page containing the selected time while preserving the account and ledger state. The result view is a replay theater: price with entry and partial-exit markers, a trade ribbon per position round, equity with high-water mark, underwater drawdown, and used margin share one time axis and one playhead; strategy-action reasons surface as the playhead passes them, and selecting a round highlights it across every track beside a one-line-per-round list. Action-to-fill association is inferred from time and labeled as such, and Calmar is marked as an estimate because the report does not store it. Backtest records expose the exact pinned strategy version. The stateful backtest contract rejects look-ahead data and implicit position reversals. Transparent K-line factors remain research evidence, not trained-model validation or trade commands.

In Strategy Research, the desktop app ships a bundled, checksum-verified CPython 3.13 runtime inside its resources and creates one Desic-owned local virtual environment from it when needed; development builds without a staged bundle fall back to a compatible Python 3.12-3.13 interpreter on PATH. That environment installs only the fixed scientific dependency set used by the source-policy allowlist and runs a strategy through the current-time JSONL contract. Python strategy processes receive no account credentials, proxy configuration, arbitrary data API, or order function; only the dependency installer exports the application's HTTP(S) proxy, so an environment can be built on a proxied network. Environment installation reports its stage, mirror index, elapsed time, and latest pip output in the research panel, keeps the complete transcript at `setup.log` and any failure record at `setup-failure.json` beside the local environment, and preserves the last failure across re-checks instead of clearing it. The local venv is dependency isolation rather than an operating-system security sandbox, so Profiles must be activated only for source the user has reviewed and trusts.

An enabled Strategy Profile pins an exact Python version, account, environment, contract, cross/isolated margin mode, target leverage, direction permissions, per-entry margin budget, same-side total margin budget, daily realized-loss limit, cooldown, and notification preference. It reuses the existing confirmed 1-minute K-line stream and fresh private-account snapshot; it does not create a second market subscription. Before evaluation, the host waits briefly for the just-closed candle to settle in local storage, repairs the complete visible history plus the recent tail when necessary, and verifies the exact confirmed cutoff again. A failed repair skips that cycle and records the real synchronization diagnostic; it never evaluates against a partial window and retries on later confirmed closes. The Python process returns only a validated open/close intent with a reason; the desktop converts eligible opens into exchange-valid contract counts from the Profile budget, instrument contract value, minimum size, lot size, equity, leverage, and existing same-side usage. It then applies Profile risk checks and sends eligible market or limit orders through the existing audited, idempotent WSS-first order route. Before entries, the host checks and, if required, synchronizes actual OKX leverage to the Profile target. Entry-attached TP/SL is supported. Profile settings expose market or trigger-after-limit execution for protection sides statically declared by the pinned strategy source; take-profit also supports a post-fill resting limit submitted immediately after actual entry fills and resized as partial fills arrive. The host never invents a missing stop-loss or take-profit. An accepted close request is only recorded as submitted; Desic does not manually cancel attached TP/SL on that acknowledgement, and OKX cancels exchange-managed attached TP/SL once the position is fully flat. Before a Profile strategy close is submitted, the host cancels that Profile's post-fill resting TP orders and marks them superseded so reconciliation cannot recreate them. Dynamic protection amendment remains disabled for live Profiles until reconciliation is complete. When an OKX account is saved or tested, the desktop verifies its position mode and automatically switches `net_mode` to `long_short_mode` when the API key can write account settings. If OKX refuses the switch because positions or orders remain, the notification center explains how to flatten and retry, and activation stays blocked. Live activation requires an exact completed backtest, local Python availability, account read/trade permission, hedge mode, conflict review against active AI automation, and an explicit confirmation. Profiles cannot be edited while running. Signal history preserves actions, blocks, order identifiers, and errors; a per-cycle runtime, snapshot, or execution error is recorded for review instead of immediately stopping the Profile. Optional Feishu delivery also obeys the global strategy-signal event setting.

Skills can additionally be installed as immutable resource bundles. For a concrete USDT linear-perpetual candidate, deterministic plan evaluation and the final exchange/account precheck distinguish gross target profit from fee-adjusted net profit. They expose the fee source, break-even price, target fee drag, fee-inclusive reward/risk, and returns relative to margin and equity while explicitly excluding unmodeled slippage and funding. Fixed-size leverage changes estimated margin and margin-relative percentages, not absolute fees or price PnL; the AI must not use higher leverage or a wider technical stop to make a weak net opportunity appear attractive. Once an entry thesis is valid without a future crossing trigger, the AI prefers a reasonably fillable resting ordinary limit order for potential maker-fee savings, while recognizing that marketable limits can still be taker orders and resting limits can miss or face adverse selection.

The AI strategy assistant remains bound to the currently selected Python strategy editor and may read, test, and update only that unsaved editor buffer through revision-checked tools. The AI trading assistant uses a separate session-scoped strategy research workspace: it may create strategies, save immutable versions, backtest, compare, and optimize strategies created in that same conversation, but it cannot read or modify an existing strategy editor or activate a Strategy Profile. Research results can be opened in Strategy Research without replacing the current editor draft.

AI Token usage is based on Provider or official CLI usage reports rather than local text-length estimates. A turn includes every model iteration plus each reported sub-Agent exactly once; cache read/write is an input breakdown rather than an additional total. Run details show cache hit rate only when both input and cache-read coverage were reported, using cache-read tokens divided by total input tokens. Missing or partial Provider usage remains visibly incomplete instead of being represented as zero, and historical local usage can be rebuilt from retained raw usage events.

Every background trading Profile reconciles watched positions with current ordinary and algo exit orders before creating new exposure. An unprotected position requires an explicit hold-and-exit condition, a protective close opportunity, or a justified reduction/close. Opposite long/short positions remain allowed as deliberate hedges only with a stated objective, size relationship, duration, unwind condition, exits, and combined portfolio risk. New Profiles use a 30-minute maximum-silence fallback by default; saved user values remain unchanged.

Every AI Automation Profile also carries hard risk limits enforced in code rather than by the model: risk per trade (default 1% of equity, fee-inclusive stop loss), minimum net reward-to-risk (default 1.2), a daily realized-loss limit after which no new entries open (default 3%, Asia/Shanghai day), at most N open positions or in-flight entries per Profile (default 2), and a maximum market-entry drift from the decision price (default 30 bps). An AI entry must carry both a stop-loss trigger and a take-profit, and may not open through a trigger (plan) order for now. A violation blocks the candidate at decision-context review and at creation; an over-budget entry is told the largest contract count its stop allows, or that the account is too small for the minimum size, and the size is never changed for the model. Right before submission the host re-reads the latest price and rejects an entry whose price has crossed the stop, a market entry that drifted beyond the limit, or any entry after the daily loss limit was hit; the opportunity is marked failed with an `execution_guard` reason. Missing equity, price or exposure data rejects the entry. Closes, cancels and amendments are never blocked by these entry rules, and manual orders and Strategy Profiles are unaffected. Wake conditions saved from the interface now wake Profiles alongside agent-written ones, and when a Profile's escalation config omits them, the nearest stop distance and margin ratio of its watched positions are derived from the fresh account snapshot so a position near its stop forces deep analysis.

AI Automation offers two kinds of Profile, chosen as two cards (Classic AI Profile and Trader Profile) when a Profile is created. The kind is fixed at creation so each Profile's run history belongs to one mode; the editor shows the kind as a read-only tag and only the settings that kind uses (shared: basics, hard risk rules, background limits; Classic adds experts and triage; Trader shows its briefing, tools and decision rules), and the Profile list can be filtered by kind. Classic mode is unchanged: the AI reads market data, intelligence and the account through tools, with experts and triage as configured. Trader mode first has code build a one-page briefing for up to three watched symbols — price changes, 15m/1h/4h structure and range position, ATR and regime, RSI, top-of-book, funding, open-interest change, taker-buy share, liquidation counts and radar rank when locally available, the account with each position's stop, take-profit and liquidation distance, today's realized PnL, remaining daily loss allowance, per-trade risk budget, exposure versus the limit, a sizing hint for a 1×ATR(1h) stop, plus the Profile's recent opportunities, last conclusions, active wake conditions and major events. Missing data is written as unavailable, never as zero, and the build is time-limited so a slow source never blocks the run. The AI then decides with one trader (no experts, no triage) and only a small set of drill-down and trading tools; the full opportunity list, intelligence, radar and account-risk tools are withheld because the briefing covers them. Hard risk rules apply identically in both modes. The mode is frozen on each run; run details show the briefing that run used, and the run history shows a Classic-vs-Trader table (runs, median tokens and duration, opportunities created and executed, closed trades and their net PnL) once trader-mode runs exist.

Strategy Profile runtime, market-data, account-snapshot, and host position-sizing failures are tracked consecutively. After three consecutive runtime failures, the affected Profile is disabled and the notification center records the automatic stop. Ordinary risk blocks such as cooldown, direction permission, or daily loss limits are audited but do not count as runtime failures.

Fastlane mode is implemented in this build but not enabled: `FASTLANE_MODE_ENABLED` is false, so its entry points stay hidden, no Profile can queue a fastlane round or start a collector, leftover queued or running fastlane rounds are cancelled with a recorded reason, and existing Profile configuration is left untouched. The mode opens in a later release.

Data page (数据) has three views. Account performance is an interactive workbench built from the user's closed positions: one large net-PnL number with a one-sentence plain-language summary, a handful of ratios (trades, win rate, profit factor, average hold, max drawdown) and a cumulative-PnL curve, over three panels — by order source, by instrument and a daily calendar. Everything is cross-filtered: click a source or instruments (multi-select), click a day, or drag a period on the curve, and the number, sentence, ratios, curve (with the whole account as a dotted comparison line), the other panels and the trade list all re-compute; active filters show as removable chips and Esc clears them. Each trade expands to entry / exit, leverage, fees and funding, and links to the full replay in Trade Review. Colours are deliberately muted and follow the red-up / green-down setting. It reads the latest 200 closed positions and says so when the chosen range is longer. Trade Review (交易复盘) is a self-check built from the user's own closed positions: at most three rule-generated findings (every one needs at least five samples and links to the trades behind it) — re-entering within 30 minutes of a losing close, trades without a stop, the steadiest and the weakest instrument — plus a weekday × time-of-day PnL heat map, win rate by leverage and with / without a stop, a filterable trade list, and a per-trade drawer with a candle replay (entry, exit, stop / take-profit lines, the four hours after exit, max adverse / favourable excursion), free tags and a private note stored only on this machine. Stop-loss information is matched from conditional-order history and is labelled as an estimate. The review never places or changes an order.

## Current Data Sync and Diagnostics Boundary

Private account history synchronizes in two phases — a shallow startup phase and a background archive phase — so the first paint no longer waits on archive endpoints, and private REST requests are paced per endpoint family (archive 400 ms, query 200 ms, others 250 ms) with unified OKX rate-limit handling that honors `Retry-After`. A startup database lock is retried only when a peer process holds the lock; permission errors and corruption still surface immediately. Synchronization notifications describe the outcome in readable form instead of echoing raw payloads. The connection board reads market and trade channels separately and adds local queue latency, rather than summing data age with snapshot age, so a backlog is reported as a backlog instead of an inflated round-trip.

## Current Desktop Update Boundary

In-app updates resolve a release from GitHub and download the installer through a redirecting asset host. A failed download retries up to three times (1 s, then 3 s) for transport errors and 5xx responses only; signature failures, missing assets, and unsupported environments fail immediately rather than being retried. When the retries are exhausted, the notice states that three attempts were made, names the asset host as the usual proxy or network cause, and links the manual download page for that version.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, professional. The interface should feel focused under sustained use and communicate risk, state, and execution outcomes without decorative noise.

## Anti-references

Avoid marketing-page composition, oversized headings, decorative cards, excessive rounding, bright ornamental gradients, and interactions that cover critical trading data without a clear reason.

## Design Principles

- Keep realtime trading information scannable and stable during rapid updates.
- Make state, permission, risk, and execution consequences explicit.
- Preserve workspace context while revealing secondary tools progressively.
- Treat news, macro events, sentiment, and smart-money activity as attributable evidence, never as an implicit trade command.
- Prefer familiar desktop-terminal interactions over novel controls.
- Keep dense workflows efficient without making the interface visually loud.

## Appearance

The default Phosphor appearance keeps color for information only (price change, buy/sell, state), reserves purple for AI surfaces, lets only live data glow, and animates only to explain change. Prices roll only the digits that changed, a one-pixel heartbeat at the top edge reflects market data freshness, ⌘K opens a command palette for workspaces, instruments, views, and asking AI (it contains no trading actions), and ⌘. opens a workspace overview. The previous Classic appearance remains selectable in Settings for one release.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Maintain readable contrast, keyboard-operable controls, non-color status cues, and reduced-motion fallbacks for every transition.
The system interface follows the operating-system language by default and supports explicit language selection, while preserving Prompt, Skill, AI-generated, user-authored, and third-party original content verbatim.
